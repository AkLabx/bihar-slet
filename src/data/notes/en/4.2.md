## 1. Nature and Characteristics of Reference Sources

> **Definition (William A. Katz):** "A reference book is a book that is compiled specifically to supply definite pieces of information of varying types in the most convenient form, intended to be consulted or referred to for specific factual data rather than read consecutively from cover to cover."

### Distinguishing Characteristics:
- **Non-Sequential Reading:** Designed for rapid factual lookups rather than continuous, linear narrative reading.
- **Systematic Architecture:** Information is strictly organized according to a predetermined scheme (most commonly alphabetical, chronological, geographic, or classified).
- **In-Library Consultation (Non-Circulating):** Reference collection materials are strictly non-circulating and restricted to reading room consultation.
- **Rich Indexing and Cross-References:** Feature comprehensive indexes, see and see-also cross-references, and analytical entries.

## 2. Encyclopedias

An encyclopedia is traditionally regarded as the **"Storehouse of All Knowledge"**. The term derives from the classical Greek phrase *"Enkyklios Paideia"*, signifying "well-rounded or comprehensive general education".

### (A) Encyclopaedia Britannica
The most prestigious, authoritative, and historically significant general English-language encyclopedia in the world.
- **First Edition:** Published in Edinburgh, Scotland, between **1768 and 1771** in **3 volumes**.
- **Founding Pioneers:** Colin Macfarquhar (printer), Andrew Bell (engraver), and William Smellie (first editor).
- **14th Edition:** 1929 (published in 24 volumes).
- **15th Edition (The New Encyclopaedia Britannica - 1974):**
  - Restructured under the intellectual leadership of **Mortimer J. Adler** into a revolutionary **tripartite system**:
    1. **Propaedia (Outline of Knowledge):** 1 volume — systematic philosophical mapping and classification of all human knowledge.
    2. **Micropaedia (Ready Reference):** 10 volumes (expanded to 12 volumes in 1985) — short, concise factual entries (under 750 words) for immediate lookups.
    3. **Macropaedia (Knowledge in Depth):** 19 volumes (revised to 17 volumes in 1985) — comprehensive, signed, exhaustive scholarly research treatises.
    4. **Two-Volume Index:** Introduced in 1985 in 2 volumes to provide detailed keyword indexing across the entire work.
- **Cessation of Print:** In March **2012**, after **244 years** of continuous print publishing, Encyclopaedia Britannica officially announced the discontinuation of its printed edition, transitioning permanently to a digital-only online platform.

### (B) Encyclopedia Americana
- **First Edition:** Published in the United States between **1829 and 1833**.
- **First Editor:** Francis Lieber.
- **Extent:** 30 volumes.
- **Specialized Strengths:** Renowned for detailed and accessible coverage of American history, biography, geography, science, and technological innovation.

### (C) Major Subject Encyclopedias
1. **Encyclopedia of Library and Information Science (ELIS):**
   - Founding Editors: Allen Kent, Harold Lancour, and Jay E. Daily.
   - Publisher: Marcel Dekker (subsequently CRC Press / Taylor & Francis).
   - First Published: 1968 onwards (extending to over 73 volumes with supplements). Regarded as the "Bible" of Library and Information Science.
2. **International Encyclopedia of the Social Sciences (IESS):**
   - Editor: David L. Sills (1968, 17 volumes; Macmillan and The Free Press).
3. **McGraw-Hill Encyclopedia of Science and Technology:**
   - 20 volumes; standard reference source for physical, life, and engineering sciences.

## 3. Dictionaries and Thesauri

A language dictionary records the words of a language, providing spelling, orthography, phonetic pronunciation, etymology, grammatical parts of speech, and definitions.

### (A) Oxford English Dictionary (OED)
- **Nature:** The definitive historical dictionary of the English language, tracing words through historical citations and textual examples (Dictionary on Historical Principles).
- **First Editor:** Sir James Augustus Henry Murray (James Murray).
- **Publication History:** Published in fascicles from 1884 to 1928 under the title *A New English Dictionary on Historical Principles (NED)* in 12 volumes plus 1 supplement.
- **Second Edition (OED2):** Published in 1989 in **20 volumes** (edited by John A. Simpson and Edmund S.C. Weiner).

### (B) Thesaurus
- The Greek word *Thesaurus* translates literally to **"treasury" or "storehouse of words"**.
- **Roget's Thesaurus of English Words and Phrases:**
  - First published in **1852** by British physician and lexicographer **Peter Mark Roget**.
  - Distinctive Structure: Words are arranged **conceptually by ideas**, grouping synonyms and antonyms together, rather than alphabetically.
- **Information Retrieval Application:** In modern library and database indexing, a thesaurus serves as a **Controlled Vocabulary** tool managing synonymous, hierarchical (Broader Term / Narrower Term), and associative (Related Term) relationships.

### (C) Specialized LIS Glossaries
- **Harrod's Librarians' Glossary and Reference Book:**
  - Original compiler: Leonard Montague Harrod (L.M. Harrod, 1938); updated by Ray Prytherch. The standard terminological reference for librarians, archivists, and records managers.

## 4. Yearbooks and Almanacs

### (A) Yearbooks (Annuals)
Compilations recording, analyzing, and presenting the political, social, scientific, and economic occurrences, trends, and statistics of the preceding calendar year.
1. **The Europa World Year Book:**
   - Established in 1926 (London, Europa Publications / Routledge).
   - Published in 2 annual volumes: Volume 1 covers international organizations and countries (A-K); Volume 2 covers countries (L-Z). Highly authoritative for comparative national statistics.
2. **The Statesman's Yearbook:**
   - Established in **1864** (Palgrave Macmillan, London). Over 160 consecutive annual editions documenting the political, diplomatic, and socioeconomic status of all nations.
3. **India: A Reference Annual:**
   - Published annually since **1953** by the Publications Division, Ministry of Information and Broadcasting, Government of India. The official compendium on Indian administrative, developmental, and governmental progress.
4. **Manorama Yearbook:** Widely circulated general knowledge annual in India (Malayala Manorama Group, Kottayam, Kerala).

### (B) Almanacs
Historically astronomical and meteorological registers (recording sunrises, tides, eclipses, calendar computations) that evolved into broad annual compilations of statistical tables and ready-reference data.
1. **Whitaker's Almanack:**
   - Founded in **1868** by Joseph Whitaker in the United Kingdom.
2. **The World Almanac and Book of Facts:**
   - Founded in **1868** by the *New York World* newspaper (USA).

## 5. Directories

A systematically ordered list of names, postal and email addresses, telephone numbers, and administrative officers of organizations, institutions, corporations, or professionals.

1. **The World of Learning:**
   - Publisher: Europa Publications (Routledge, London).
   - Published annually since **1947**. The premier global directory of universities, colleges, research institutes, learned societies, libraries, and museums worldwide.
2. **Universities Handbook:**
   - Published by the **Association of Indian Universities (AIU)**, New Delhi.
   - Comprehensive official directory detailing recognized universities, university-level institutions, faculties, and academic programs across India.
3. **Yellow Pages:** Classified commercial directories of business enterprises and trade suppliers.

## 6. Biographical Sources

Biographical reference sources supply authenticated facts regarding individuals' birth, parentage, educational qualifications, career appointments, honors, published works, and achievements.

### Biographical Sources Typology:
```
                       Biographical Sources
                            /          \
                           /            \
          Current (Living Persons)        Retrospective (Deceased Persons)
       - Who's Who (1849, UK)             - Who Was Who (UK)
       - Who's Who in America (1899)      - Dictionary of National Biography (DNB)
       - India Who's Who (INFA)           - Webster's Biographical Dictionary
```

### Premier Biographical Works:
1. **Who's Who (UK):**
   - Published annually since **1849** (A&C Black, London). Restricted exclusively to distinguished **living personalities**.
2. **Who Was Who:**
   - Cumulative retrospective volumes containing biographies removed from *Who's Who* upon the biographical subjects' decease, updated with their date of death.
3. **Dictionary of National Biography (DNB - UK):**
   - Founded in **1885** by George Smith; edited by Leslie Stephen and Sir Sidney Lee. Standard monumental multi-volume record of deceased British historical figures.
4. **Dictionary of National Biography (DNB - India):**
   - Edited by Dr. S.P. Sen in **4 volumes (1972–1974)**; published by the Institute of Historical Studies, Kolkata. Covers over 1,400 eminent nationalist figures of the Indian freedom struggle and nation-building (1800–1947).
5. **India Who's Who:** Published annually by INFA Publications (New Delhi), covering notable living Indian leaders across professions.

## 7. Geographical Sources

Reference tools answering queries regarding place names, geographical locations, travel distances, administrative boundaries, topographical features, and populations:

### (A) Gazetteers (Geographical Dictionaries)
An alphabetical dictionary of geographic names providing coordinates, latitude/longitude, historical background, administration, physical topography, and economic activities.
1. **The Imperial Gazetteer of India:**
   - Conceived and initiated by **Sir William Wilson Hunter** (First edition 1881 in 9 volumes; second edition 1885–87 in 14 volumes).
   - **Monumental New Edition (1907–1909):** Published in **26 volumes** by the Clarendon Press, Oxford (Volumes 1–4: The Indian Empire; Volumes 5–24: Alphabetical Gazetteer; Volume 25: Index; Volume 26: Atlas).
2. **The Columbia Gazetteer of the World:** Edited by Saul B. Cohen (Columbia University Press).
3. **Webster's New Geographical Dictionary:** Merriam-Webster.

### (B) Maps, Atlases, and Globes
- **Atlas:** A bound volume of maps. The term *Atlas* was first applied to a collection of maps by **Gerardus Mercator** in 1595.
- **The Times Comprehensive Atlas of the World:** The world's benchmark cartographic atlas.
- **National Atlas and Thematic Mapping Organisation (NATMO):**
  - Located in Kolkata; established in **1956** under the guidance of Prof. S.P. Chatterjee. Operates under the Department of Science and Technology, Government of India; creates the official *National Atlas of India*.

### (C) Travel Guides (Guidebooks)
- Practical handbooks containing maps, accommodation details, cultural sights, currency regulations, and transportation for travelers.
- *Examples:* **Baedeker Guides** (pioneered by Karl Baedeker in 1827 in Germany), **Lonely Planet**, **Fodor's Guides**.

## 8. Criteria for Evaluation of Reference Sources

Pioneering library scholars **Louis Shores** and **William A. Katz** established **six fundamental criteria** for the systematic appraisal and acquisition of reference sources:

1. **Authority:** Reputation, academic credentials, and subject competence of the author, compiler, editorial board, and publishing house.
2. **Scope:** Statement of purpose, intended target audience, subject breadth, depth of coverage, and chronological/geographic parameters.
3. **Treatment:** Accuracy, objectivity, clarity, style, reliability, and absence of ideological bias.
4. **Arrangement:** Systematic sequence of entries (alphabetical, chronological, or classified), cross-references (see and see-also), and indexing quality.
5. **Format:** Physical construction, paper quality, binding durability, typography, illustrations, plates, and electronic interface accessibility.
6. **Special Features & Recency:** Unique reference tables, appendixes, revision frequency, and currency of statistical data.
