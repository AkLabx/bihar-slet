### Part 1: Renowned Folk Painting Traditions of Bihar

| Painting Tradition | Origin & Geographic Region | Distinctive Features & Artistic Techniques | Renowned Master Artists & Key Facts |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Madhubani / Mithila Painting** | Mithilanchal (Madhubani, Darbhanga, Sitamarhi) | • Indigenous folk art traditionally practiced by women.<br>• Two primary classical formats: **Bhitti Chitra** (Mural Wall Paintings) and **Aripana** (Floor artwork created with rice-paste).<br>• Bhitti Chitra is divided into two sanctums: **Gosain Ghar** (Religious/Family deity room) and **Kohbar Ghar** (Nuptial chamber).<br>• Kohbar motifs symbolize fertility and longevity: Bamboo (lineage continuity), Lotus and Fish (fertility, prosperity), Tortoise (longevity), Sun, and Moon.<br>• Executed using natural pigments (turmeric, indigo, pollen, soot) with bamboo twigs and fingers. Holds prestigious **GI Tag** status. | • **Padma Shri Jagdamba Devi** (First artist to achieve national recognition in 1975).<br>• Padma Shri Sita Devi, Mahasundari Devi, Godavari Dutta, and Bharti Dayal.<br>• **Dulari Devi** (Honoured with Padma Shri in 2021). |
| **Manjusha Art (Angika / Snake Painting)** | Anga Region (Bhagalpur & adjoining tracts) | • Rooted in the ancient folkloric myth of Bihula-Bishahari.<br>• Known as **'Angika Painting'** or **'Snake Art'** due to the dominant symbolism of serpents.<br>• Historically rendered on handcrafted bamboo and reed containers/boxes (*Manjushas*).<br>• **Strictly restricted to three primary colours:** **Pink, Green, and Yellow**.<br>• Highly stylized linear and wavy geometric patterns. | • Chakravarty Devi (Legendary master folk painter).<br>• Nirmala Devi.<br>• Prominently featured across Bhagalpur silks, railway mural installations, and state handicrafts. |
| **Patna Kalam (Company School of Painting)** | Patna (Post-Mughal collapse, 18th to mid-20th century) | • Also referred to as **'Company Style'**; historically dominated by male artisans.<br>• **Theme:** Intimate depiction of ordinary daily livelihood and artisans (carpenters, washermen, blacksmiths, butchers, palanquin bearers, fishmongers).<br>• **Signature Technique:** **'Kajli Syahi'** — painting fine miniature contours directly with camel-hair brushes without prior pencil sketches.<br>• Rendered on handmade paper, ivory plates, and mica sheets. | • **Sewak Ram** (First celebrated maestro of the style).<br>• Hulas Lal, Shiv Lal, Jhumak Lal.<br>• **Ishwari Prasad Verma** (The last great representative master of Patna Kalam). |

### Part 2: Famous Fairs & Traditional Folk Festivals of Bihar

#### A. Chhath Puja (Mahaparva)
* **Cultural Primacy:** The most revered, sacred, and identity-defining folk festival of Bihar, epitomizing purity, environmental conservation, and deep cosmic reverence.
* **Frequency:** Observed biannually (the primary **Kartiki Chhath** during Shukla Shashthi of Kartika month, and **Chaiti Chhath** during Chaitra month).
* **Deities:** Dedicated to the visible solar deity **Surya (Sun God)** and his celestial consort/sister **Chhathi Maiya (Goddess Usha/Shashthi)**. It symbolizes cosmic harmony, where reverence is offered to the setting sun first, followed by the rising sun.
* **Four-Day Sacred Ritual Sequence:**
  1. *Day 1:* **Nahay-Khay** (Ritualistic purification bath followed by a satvik meal of bottle-gourd curry and rice).
  2. *Day 2:* **Kharna** (Rigorous day-long waterless fast concluded in the evening with offerings of jaggery kheer, rotis, and banana).
  3. *Day 3:* **Sandhya Arghya** (Standing in natural river water at sunset to offer libations using bamboo winnowing trays [*Soop*] filled with fruits and Thekua).
  4. *Day 4:* **Usha Arghya / Paran** (Offering morning oblations to the rising sun, successfully concluding the arduous 36-hour nirjala fast).
* **Mahaprasad:** **Thekua** — traditional cookie-confection prepared from whole wheat flour, pure ghee, and jaggery/sugar, seasoned with cardamom.

#### B. Sonepur Cattle Fair (Harihar Kshetra Mela)
* **Location:** Sonepur (Saran district), at the sacred **confluence of the Ganga and Gandak (Narayani)** rivers.
* **Duration:** Begins annually on Kartika Purnima (November–December) and continues for roughly a month.
* **Significance:** **Asia's largest cattle and livestock fair**. Locally known as 'Harihar Kshetra Mela' or 'Chhattar Mela'.
* **Historical Pedigree:** Historically, Mauryan Emperor Chandragupta Maurya and later Sher Shah Suri procured elephants and warhorses from Sonepur for their imperial armies.

#### C. Other Celebrated Fairs
* **Pitru Paksha Fair (Gaya):** Organized during the dark fortnight of Ashvina on the sacred **banks of the Falgu river**. Millions of pilgrims from India and abroad congregate to perform **Pinda Daan and Tarpan** rites for ancestral salvation. The sanctum of Vishnupad Temple (reconstructed in 1787 by Maharani Ahilyabai Holkar of Indore) is the focal center.
* **Malmas / Adhimas Fair (Rajgir):** Held every three years during the leap lunar month (Adhimas). Devotees take ritual purification dips in Rajgir's natural thermal hot sulphur springs (*Saptadhara* and *Brahmakund*).
* **Saurath Sabha (Madhubani):** Historic annual congregation in Saurath village where Maithil Brahmin families gather to negotiate and finalize marriages based on certified genealogical scrolls preserved by hereditary genealogists (*Panjikars*).
* **Bansi Mela:** Celebrated with traditional fervor at the foot of Mandar Hill (Banka district) on Makar Sankranti.

### Part 3: UNESCO World Heritage & Prime Tourism Sites

#### A. UNESCO World Heritage Sites in Bihar
1. **Mahabodhi Temple Complex at Bodh Gaya (Inscribed in 2002):**
   * First temple erected by Mauryan Emperor Ashoka in the 3rd century BCE; current grand brick temple structure dates from the classical Gupta era.
   * Houses the sacred **Bodhi Tree (Ficus religiosa)** beneath which Prince Siddhartha Gautama achieved supreme enlightenment at age 35 to become the **Buddha**.
2. **Archaeological Site of Nalanda Mahavihara (Inscribed in 2016):**
   * Founded in the 5th century CE under the patronage of Gupta Emperor **Kumaragupta I**.
   * The world's earliest renowned residential international university. Chinese pilgrim-scholar Xuanzang (Hiuen Tsang) studied here; its venerable abbot at the time was Acharya Shilabhadra.

#### B. Major Historical, Religious & Heritage Monuments
* **Vaishali:** Capital of the ancient **Vajji Confederacy (Licchavis)**, recognized as the world's first republic; birthplace of Lord Mahavira (Kundalpur); site of Lord Buddha's last sermon; host to the Second Buddhist Council (383 BCE under Kalashoka).
* **Pawapuri (Nalanda):** Sacred nirvana (salvation) site of Lord Mahavira, the 24th Jain Tirthankara. Features the stunning white marble **Jal Mandir** standing amidst an expanse of pink lotuses.
* **Rajgir (Girivraja):** Ancient capital of the Magadha Empire. Notable for Bimbisara's Prison, Gridhrakuta Peak (Vishwa Shanti Stupa), Venuvana, and hot springs.
* **Takht Sri Harmandir Ji Patna Sahib:** Holy birthplace of the 10th Sikh Guru, **Guru Gobind Singh Ji** (Born December 22, 1666). One of the five sacred Takhts of Sikhism, constructed by Maharaja Ranjit Singh.
* **Kesariya Stupa (East Champaran):** The tallest Buddhist stupa in the world, rising to a height of 104 feet.
* **Golghar (Patna):** Massive cylindrical pillarless granary constructed in 1786 by British engineer **Captain John Garstin** following the catastrophic Bengal famine of 1770.
* **Mausoleum of Sher Shah Suri (Sasaram):** Architectural masterpiece of Indo-Islamic design, built of red Chunar sandstone in the center of an artificial octagonal lake (Chief Architect: Mir Muhammad Aliwal Khan).

### Part 5: Exam Revision Summary Pointers

> [!IMPORTANT]
> 1. **Madhubani Art:** Mural walls (Gosain Ghar & Kohbar Ghar) + Aripana floor patterns. Padma Shri recipients include Jagdamba Devi (1975) and Dulari Devi (2021).
> 2. **Manjusha Art:** Restricted to exactly three colours: Pink, Green, and Yellow. Bhagalpur region, rooted in the Bihula-Bishahari legend.
> 3. **Patna Kalam:** Company School, Kajli Syahi brush technique without preliminary pencil lines, portraits of everyday commoners. First master: Sewak Ram; last master: Ishwari Prasad.
> 4. **UNESCO Sites:** Mahabodhi Temple Complex (2002) and Nalanda Mahavihara (2016).
> 5. **Bharat Ratna Recipients associated with Bihar:** Dr. Rajendra Prasad (1962), Dr. Zakir Husain (1963), Maulana Abul Kalam Azad (1992), Jayaprakash Narayan (1999), Ustad Bismillah Khan (2001), and Karpoori Thakur (2024).
> 6. **Dinkar:** Awarded Jnanpith in 1972 for Urvashi; Sahitya Akademi in 1959 for Sanskriti Ke Chaar Adhyay. Born at Simariya (Begusarai).
