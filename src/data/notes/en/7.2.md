### Part 2: Physiographic Divisions & Geomorphology

Bihar is divided into three distinct physiographic regions:

#### A. Sub-Himalayan Terai / Siwalik Foothill Region
* Confined to the extreme north-western corner in **West Champaran** district (covering ~932 sq km).
* **Someshwar Range:** The highest elevation point in Bihar (**874 meters**).
* **Dun Valley (Ramnagar Dun):** Longitudinal valley located south of Someshwar Range composed of coarse gravel, alluvium, and pebbles.

#### B. The Great Gangetic Plain of Bihar
* Encompasses nearly **95% of Bihar's total geographical landmass**, created by Pleistocene to Recent alluvial silt deposited by the Ganga and its tributaries.
* **North Bihar Plain:** Extends from the Himalayan Terai to the northern bank of the Ganga. It is a flood-prone alluvial terrain characterized by **Doabs**, oxbow lakes (*Chaur* and *Maan*), and vast meandering river channels.
* **South Bihar Plain:** Lies between the southern bank of the Ganga and the Chota Nagpur plateau margins. Marked by natural levees (*tatas*) and backwater depression basins locally designated as **Tal lands** (e.g., Mokama Tal, Barahiya Tal).

#### C. Southern Plateau Fringe
* **Dharwar Rock Formations:** Found in Munger, Jamui, Nawada, and Gaya (the oldest Precambrian rock structures in Bihar), hosting mica, quartzites, and phyllites.
* **Vindhyan Rock Formations:** Prominent in Kaimur and Rohtas districts (rich in sandstone, limestone, and pyrite beds, supporting cement manufacturing).

### Part 3: Drainage System & River Basins

The **Ganga River** serves as the master drainage artery of Bihar, bisecting the state horizontally from west to east.

```
                           Himalayan Rivers (Nepal)
     [Ghaghara]  [Gandak]  [Burhi Gandak]  [Bagmati]  [Kosi (Sorrow)]  [Mahananda]
         ↓          ↓           ↓              ↓            ↓               ↓
───────────────────────────── Ganga River (445 km) ──────────────────────────→ Bay of Bengal
         ↑          ↑           ↑              ↑            ↑               ↑
     [Karmanasa]  [Son]      [Punpun]       [Falgu]      [Kiul]         [Chandan]
                     Peninsular Plateau Rivers (South)
```

#### A. The Ganga River
* **Entry Point:** Enters Bihar near **Chausa (Buxar district)**.
* **Length:** Total length is 2,525 km; **length within Bihar is 445 km**.
* **Districts Traversed:** Flows through **12 districts** (maximum course of **99 km in Patna district**).
* **Exit Point:** Exits Bihar downstream of Manihari (Katihar district) into West Bengal.

#### B. North-Bank (Left-Bank) Himalayan Tributaries
1. **Ghaghara (Saryu):** Originates from Mapchachungo glacier (Tibet) and joins the Ganga at the border of Siwan and Saran (near Revelganj/Chapra).
2. **Gandak (Narayani / Saligrami):** Originates in Nepal Himalayas; enters Bihar at Valmikinagar (Bhainsalotan barrage) and joins the Ganga near **Sonepur (Hajipur)**.
3. **Burhi Gandak:** Originates from Someshwar hills (Chautarwa Chaur); holds the **fastest surface flow velocity** among Bihar rivers; merges with the Ganga near Khagaria.
4. **Bagmati & Kamla:** Flow from Nepal's Mahabharat Range, traversing North Bihar and draining into the Kosi river network.
5. **Kosi (Saptakoshi):** Originates in Tibet/Nepal (Gosainthan); notorious for rampant sediment deposition, devastating annual floods, and shifting its course over 120 km in two centuries; globally renowned as the **'Sorrow of Bihar'**; joins the Ganga at **Kursela (Katihar)**.
6. **Mahananda:** Originates from Darjeeling hills (West Bengal), drains Kishanganj, Purnia, and Katihar, and forms the easternmost river system of Bihar.

#### C. South-Bank (Right-Bank) Peninsular Tributaries
1. **Karmanasa:** Originates from the Kaimur Hills; joins the Ganga near Chausa; historically deemed an **inauspicious/unholy river** in local folklore.
2. **Son River:** Originates from Amarkantak plateau (Madhya Pradesh); enters Bihar at Rohtas and merges with the Ganga near **Maner (Danapur/Patna)**. The historic Son Canal system (Dehri-on-Son, 1874) is Bihar's oldest canal network.
3. **Punpun:** Originates from Chauraha hill (Palamu, Jharkhand) and joins the Ganga at **Fatuha (Patna)**.
4. **Falgu (Niranjana):** Formed by the union of Lilajan and Mohana rivers near Bodh Gaya. Gautama Buddha attained supreme enlightenment beneath the Bodhi tree on its banks. It is the sacred site for Pinda Daan rites during Pitru Paksha.

### Part 4: Agro-Climatic Zones & Soil Types

#### A. Climate & Agro-Climatic Classification
* Climate type: **Sub-tropical Humid Continental Monsoon**.
* Koppen's climate classification: North Bihar is classified as **Cwg**; South Bihar as **Aw / Cwg**.
* **District with Highest Rainfall:** **Kishanganj** (due to direct exposure to the Bay of Bengal branch of the Southwest Monsoon).
* **District with Lowest Rainfall:** **Aurangabad / Arwal**.
* **Hottest & Coldest District:** **Gaya** (influenced by continental terrain and proximity to rocky plateaus).

#### B. Major Soil Types
1. **Khadar (Newer Alluvium):** Found in floodplains of North Bihar (Purnia, Saharsa, Darbhanga, Muzaffarpur); exceptionally fertile, enriched annually by flood silts; rich in potash, deficient in nitrogen and phosphorus.
2. **Bhangar (Older Alluvium):** Upland alluvial plains elevated above annual flood limits (Shahabad, Gaya, Patna plains).
3. **Karail-Kewal Soil:** Heavy, fine-textured, highly tenacious clayey soil prevalent in South Bihar plains, suited for rabi crops (wheat, pulses).
4. **Balthar Soil:** Coarse-grained, yellow-reddish acidic soil found along the contact zone between the southern alluvial plain and the Chota Nagpur plateau fringe.

### Part 5: Forest Cover, Biodiversity & Wildlife Sanctuaries

* **Forest Cover (India State of Forest Report - ISFR 2021):**
  * Total Forest Cover of Bihar: **7,380.79 sq km (7.84% of total state area)**.
  * Tree Cover: 2,341 sq km (2.49%).
  * **District with Maximum Forest Area & Percentage:** **Kaimur (1,051.56 sq km - 31.56%)**, followed by West Champaran.
  * **District with Minimum Forest Area & Percentage:** **Sheikhpura (1.19 sq km - 0.17%)**.
* **National Park:**
  * **Valmiki National Park:** West Champaran; **Bihar's sole National Park and Tiger Reserve**.
* **Key Wildlife Sanctuaries & Ramsar Wetlands:**
  * **Kabar Tal (Kanwar Lake):** Begusarai; **Bihar's first Ramsar Site (39th in India)**; Asia's largest freshwater oxbow lake formed by the meandering Gandak.
  * **Vikramshila Gangetic Dolphin Sanctuary:** Bhagalpur (Gangetic Dolphin, *Platanista gangetica*, is India's National Aquatic Animal).
  * **Kakolat Waterfall:** Nawada district (scenic waterfall falling from 150-160 feet).
  * **Bhimbandh Wildlife Sanctuary:** Munger (renowned for natural thermal hot sulphur springs).
  * **Gautam Buddha Wildlife Sanctuary:** Gaya.

### Part 6: Mineral Wealth of Bihar

Following the creation of Jharkhand in 2000, primary metallic mineral belts transitioned south. However, Bihar preserves massive reserves of non-metallic, construction, and strategic industrial minerals:

| Mineral | Major Producing / Reserve Areas | Crucial Examination Facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Pyrites** | **Amjhore (Rohtas)** | Bihar possesses **~95% of India's total pyrite reserves**. Essential raw material for sulfuric acid and chemical fertilizers. |
| **Limestone** | Rohtas and Kaimur Hills | Forms the industrial raw material backbone for cement factories at Banjari and Dalmianagar. |
| **Gold Reserves** | **Sono Block (Jamui)** | Geological Survey of India (GSI) confirmed Jamui holds India's largest primary gold ore deposit (~44% of national gold reserves, ~222.88 million tonnes). |
| **Mica** | Gaya, Nawada (Rajauli), Jamui | High-quality ruby mica occurring in pegmatite veins. |
| **Uranium** | Gaya | Strategic radioactive mineral traces. |
| **China Clay (Kaolin)** | Bhagalpur and Banka | Used in high-grade ceramic and porcelain manufacturing. |
| **Bauxite** | Kharagpur Hills (Munger) | Aluminum ore reserves. |
