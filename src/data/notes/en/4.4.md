## 1. Document Delivery Service (DDS)

> **Definition:** Document Delivery Service (DDS) is the provision of a published document (journal article, research report, book chapter, conference paper, or patent), either in its original physical form or as a surrogate (photocopy, microform, or electronic digital PDF), directly to a requesting patron upon explicit demand.

### Traditional DDS vs. Electronic DDS (EDDS):
- **Traditional DDS:** Physical dispatch of printed articles, paper photocopies, or loan copies via postal mail, parcel courier, or personal in-person pick-up.
- **EDDS (Electronic Document Delivery Service):** Direct end-to-end digital transmission of scanned documents, high-resolution PDFs, or electronic files via secure web portals, encrypted FTP, Ariel software, or email attachments, drastically compressing turnaround time from weeks to hours.

### BLDSC and Major Global Supply Centres:
1. **British Library Document Supply Centre (BLDSC):**
   - **Location:** Boston Spa, Wetherby, West Yorkshire, United Kingdom.
   - **Historical Evolution:** Established in **1973** (evolved from the National Lending Library for Science and Technology - NLLST, founded by Dr. Donald J. Urquhart in 1962).
   - **Global Significance:** The world's largest, most comprehensive document supply center, processing millions of international scientific and technical requests annually for libraries across the globe.

### Indian Document Delivery Infrastructure:
1. **CSIR-NIScPR (National Institute of Science Communication and Policy Research, New Delhi):**
   - Formerly **INSDOC** (merged with NISCOM to form NISCAIR, now CSIR-NIScPR).
   - Functions as India's premier National Document Delivery Centre through the holdings of the **National Science Library (NSL)**.
2. **DELNET (Developing Library Network, New Delhi):**
   - Network connecting over 8,000 institutional libraries across India and South Asia, facilitating extensive Inter-Library Loan and electronic document delivery.
3. **INFLIBNET Centre (Gandhinagar):**
   - Coordinates resource sharing and document supply among Indian central and state universities under the **e-ShodhSindhu** and Inter-Library Resource Sharing consortia.

## 2. Inter-Library Loan (ILL)

> **Concept:** Guided by Dr. S.R. Ranganathan's **Fifth Law of Library Science ("A Library is a Growing Organism")** and the economic reality that no single library can achieve absolute self-sufficiency, **Inter-Library Loan (ILL)** is a formal agreement whereby a library borrows books or documents from another independent library to satisfy a specific loan request of its registered user.

### The Indispensable Role of Union Catalogues:
- An ILL network cannot function without a centralized locating tool: the **Union Catalogue**.
- **Definition:** A combined, unified catalog listing the holding records of books, serials, or theses across two or more autonomous libraries within a defined region, network, or nation.
- Without a union catalogue, a librarian cannot determine which partner institution holds the requested item.
- **Key Union Catalogues in India:**
  - **IndCat:** Online Union Catalogue of Indian Universities developed and maintained by INFLIBNET Centre, listing millions of books, theses, and serial titles.
  - **DELNET Union Catalogue:** Integrated union databases of books, periodicals, and non-book materials held by participating member institutions.

### IFLA Voucher Scheme:
- Developed by the International Federation of Library Associations and Institutions (IFLA) in **1995**.
- Purpose: Eliminates financial barriers, currency exchange fluctuations, banking fees, and international transaction costs associated with cross-border ILL and photocopying payments.
- Participating libraries purchase standardized reusable plastic coupons (vouchers) from IFLA and exchange them directly as universal international library currency.

## 3. User Education and Information Literacy

### Patricia B. Knapp and the Monteith College Experiment (1966):
- **Pioneer of User Education:** **Patricia B. Knapp**.
- **Monteith College Library Experiment (Wayne State University, Detroit, USA, 1966):**
  - A landmark research project demonstrating that integrating library instruction and bibliographical search methods directly into college academic curricula produces superior student critical thinking, independent learning, and scholastic achievement.

### Hierarchical Levels of User Education:
Modern library pedagogues classify user education into **three developmental tiers**:

```
                         Levels of User Education
                                     |
         +---------------------------+---------------------------+
         |                                                       |
 1. Library Orientation /      2. Bibliographic Instruction       3. Information Literacy
    Initiation of a Freshman      (BI)                              (IL)
 - Physical tour of building   - How to use OPAC / Catalogs       - Recognizing information need
 - Introduction to sections    - Searching subject databases      - Critical evaluation of sources
 - Rules, hours, membership    - Indexes and bibliographies       - Ethical, legal utilization
```

1. **Library Orientation / Initiation of a Freshman:**
   - Termed **"Initiation of a Freshman"** by Dr. S.R. Ranganathan.
   - Conducted at the commencement of an academic term to introduce newly enrolled students to the physical layout, reading rooms, circulation desks, lending rules, operating hours, and basic library staff assistance.
2. **Bibliographic Instruction (BI):**
   - Formal instructional programs designed to teach students how to execute targeted literature searches, operate the OPAC, navigate subject bibliographies, interrogate abstracting journals, and utilize electronic citation databases.
3. **Information Literacy (IL):**
   - Higher-order cognitive capability enabling individuals to identify when information is needed, and to locate, evaluate, synthesize, and ethically use that information.

### Information Literacy: Concept and Definition:
- **Origin of Term:** Coined in **1974** by **Paul G. Zurkowski**, then President of the Information Industry Association (USA), in a proposal submitted to the National Commission on Libraries and Information Science (NCLIS).
- **Classic American Library Association (ALA) Definition (1989):**
  > "To be information literate, a person must be able to recognize when information is needed and have the ability to locate, evaluate, and use effectively the needed information."

### SCONUL Seven Pillars of Information Literacy:
Formulated in **1999** by the Society of College, National and University Libraries (SCONUL) in the United Kingdom:
1. **Identify:** Ability to recognize personal information need.
2. **Scope:** Ability to assess current knowledge and identify knowledge gaps.
3. **Plan:** Ability to construct strategies for locating information and data.
4. **Gather:** Ability to locate and access the information and data required.
5. **Evaluate:** Ability to review the research process and critically appraise data and sources for reliability and bias.
6. **Manage:** Ability to organize information professionally and ethically (citation management, avoiding plagiarism).
7. **Present:** Ability to synthesize and communicate findings effectively to targeted audiences.

## 4. Library Public Relations and Information Marketing

### Library Public Relations (PR):
Public Relations is the planned, continuous management effort to establish and maintain mutual understanding, goodwill, and high institutional reputation between a library and its user community (patrons, faculty, students, administration, funding agencies).
- **Principal PR Instruments:**
  - **Library Website & Digital Portals:** Dynamic updates, announcements, resource discovery links.
  - **Book Exhibitions:** Arranged during National Library Week, World Book Day, or commemorative birth anniversaries of historical thinkers.
  - **Library Brochures and Newsletters:** Descriptive printed guides outlining library policies, collections, and emerging services.
  - **Annual Report:** Official statutory document summarizing library activities, circulation statistics, acquisitions, budget expenditures, and milestones.

### Marketing of Information Services: Kotler's 4 Ps:
Applying modern marketing management principles ensures that library resources are actively promoted as valuable products satisfying explicit patron needs:

#### Philip Kotler's 4 Ps in Library Context:
| Marketing Mix (4 Ps) | Classical Commercial Concept | Library & Information Science Application |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Product** | Physical manufactured commodity | Books, journals, electronic databases, CAS, SDI, reference guidance |
| **Price** | Monetary cost | User's time, physical travel effort, cognitive exertion, membership fee |
| **Place** | Distribution channels / outlets | Physical library building, reading rooms, OPAC terminals, remote proxy |
| **Promotion** | Commercial advertising / sales | User orientation sessions, website banners, social media alerts, emails |

### User Studies and Investigative Methods:
Scientific inquiries into patrons' actual information needs, browsing behaviors, and reading habits:
- **Methods:** Questionnaires, Structured Interviews, Direct Observation, Diary Studies, and Citation Analysis of faculty/student theses.
