## 1. Conceptual Foundation: DIKW Hierarchy

In Library and Information Science, understanding the information lifecycle requires mastery of the **DIKW Pyramid (Data-Information-Knowledge-Wisdom)**:

```
          / \
         /   \        Wisdom: Practical application of knowledge with ethical judgment
        /     \
       / Knowledge \   Knowledge: Structured, assimilated, and actionable information
      / Information \  Information: Processed and contextualized data with meaning
     /     Data      \ Data: Raw, unorganized symbols, figures, and elementary facts
    /_________________\
```

| Level | Definition | Academic Character | Exemplar |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Data** | Raw, unorganized, context-free figures, symbols, or uninterpreted observations. | Syntactic units lacking context or direct utility. | `2026`, `LET`, `150` |
| **Information** | Data that has been processed, organized, structured, and presented within a meaningful context. | Semantically enriched data answering questions of *Who, What, Where, When*. | "The Bihar Librarian Eligibility Test 2026 comprises 150 questions." |
| **Knowledge** | Actionable understanding synthesized through cognition, education, analysis, and experience. | Cognitive synthesis answering *How* and *Why*; rules and patterns. | "Application of classification and cataloguing codes to organize library collections." |
| **Wisdom** | Judicious and ethical application of knowledge to make optimal, high-level decisions. | Evaluative judgment addressing long-term goals and human values. | "Strategic collection development balancing student curriculum and budget." |

## 2. Classification Systems of Information Sources

Information resources are categorized based on their degree of originality, immediacy of observation, and physical packaging by leading library scientists:

### (A) Dennis Grogan's Tripartite Classification (1966)
In his authoritative treatise *"Case Studies in Reference Work"* (1966), Dennis Grogan divided all documentary information sources into **three distinct categories**:
1. **Primary Sources:** Contain original research, direct empirical observations, new interpretations, or first-hand discovery.
2. **Secondary Sources:** Compiled from, based upon, or leading to primary sources; they organize, distill, index, and repackage primary literature.
3. **Tertiary Sources:** Compiled from primary and secondary materials; they serve as signposts and navigational aids to guide searchers to primary and secondary documents.

### (B) C.W. Hanson's Bipartite Classification (1971)
In *"Introduction to Science-Information Work"* (1971), C.W. Hanson classified documentary sources into only **two major categories**:
1. **Primary Sources:** Documents containing original, unorganized scientific information.
2. **Secondary Sources:** Encompasses all secondary compilations as well as tertiary guidebooks and directories (subsuming Grogan's secondary and tertiary into a single overarching secondary class).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Key Exam Distinction:** If asked who classified information sources into **three groups (Primary, Secondary, Tertiary)**, the answer is **Dennis Grogan (1966)**. If asked who classified sources into **two groups (Primary and Secondary)**, the answer is **C.W. Hanson (1971)**.

### (C) Dr. S.R. Ranganathan's Documentary Classification
Dr. Ranganathan categorized documents based on their physical characteristics, recording media, and technological refinement into **four classes**:
1. **Conventional Documents:** Standard printed paper documents (e.g., printed books, serials, maps, pamphlets).
2. **Neo-conventional Documents:** Micro-documents recording nascent micro-thought on conventional media with specialized technical formats (e.g., patents, standards, specifications, technical data sheets).
3. **Non-conventional Documents:** Non-paper media requiring mechanical or optical equipment for reproduction and reading (e.g., microfilms, microcards, microfiches, audio-visual tapes).
4. **Meta-documents:** Direct instrumental recordings bypassing direct human sensory observation (e.g., satellite telemetry, radar recordings, direct scientific instrument plate outputs).

## 3. Primary Sources of Information

> **Definition:** Primary sources are firsthand documentary materials created during the original observation, experimentation, or intellectual creation. They are the initial points of dissemination and have not been filtered, condensed, or evaluated by secondary compilers.

### Detailed Breakdown of Primary Sources:

#### 1. Research Periodicals and Scholarly Journals
- The principal conduit for transmitting nascent scientific and scholarly micro-thought.
- **The First Scholarly Journals in History (1665):**
  - **Journal des Scavans** (Paris, January 5, 1665) — The world's first scholarly journal, founded by Denis de Sallo.
  - **Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society** (London, March 6, 1665) — The first scientific journal exclusively dedicated to natural science, founded by Henry Oldenburg.
- Articles undergo rigorous peer review prior to publication.

#### 2. Theses and Dissertations
- Comprehensive, original academic research submitted to a university for the award of a doctoral (Ph.D.) or master's degree.
- Represent authoritative, in-depth investigations into specific unexplored problem domains.
- **National Digital Repository in India:** **Shodhganga** (administered by INFLIBNET Centre, Gandhinagar).

#### 3. Conference Proceedings
- Compilations of research papers presented at national or international conferences, symposia, and colloquia.
- Often disseminate nascent discoveries months or years before formal appearance in peer-reviewed journals.

#### 4. Patents
- An exclusive legal monopoly granted by a sovereign government to an inventor for a limited period in exchange for full public disclosure of the invention.
- Contains the earliest, highly detailed technical blueprints and procedural steps of industrial processes.
- **Indian Patent Legislation:** Indian Patents Act, 1970 (amended in 2005 to cover both product and process patents; patent term is **20 years** from the filing date).

#### 5. Standards and Specifications
- Normative documents established by consensus and approved by a recognized standardization authority that prescribe rules, guidelines, or characteristics for products or operations.
- **Key Standardization Authorities:**
  - **BIS (Bureau of Indian Standards):** National standard body of India, established under BIS Act 1986 (successor to the Indian Standards Institution - ISI, founded in 1947).
  - **ISO (International Organization for Standardization):** Geneva, Switzerland; founded on February 23, 1947.

#### 6. Trade Literature
- Catalogues, brochures, technical data sheets, and manuals published by manufacturers and commercial enterprises describing products, operation procedures, and specifications.

#### 7. Other Primary Materials:
- Official government gazettes, court law reports, archival manuscripts, diaries, lab notebooks, correspondence, and unclassified technical project reports.

## 4. Secondary Sources of Information

> **Definition:** Secondary sources are compiled from, analyze, reorganize, index, or summarize primary sources. Their primary function is to bring scattered primary literature under systematic bibliographic control and enable efficient retrospective retrieval.

### Detailed Breakdown of Secondary Sources:

#### 1. Indexing and Abstracting Periodicals
- Essential secondary services designed to cope with the global information explosion.
- **Indexing Periodicals:** Provide systematic alphabetical or classified bibliographic citations (author, title, journal, volume, pages) without summaries (e.g., *Guide to Indian Periodical Literature*).
- **Abstracting Periodicals:** Provide complete bibliographic citations supplemented by a concise, objective summary (abstract) of the research paper.
  - *Classic Global Examples:* **Chemical Abstracts** (founded in 1907 by the American Chemical Society), **Biological Abstracts** (1926), **Library and Information Science Abstracts (LISA)** (founded in 1969).

#### 2. Bibliographies
- Organized, systematic lists of books, monographs, and other documents arranged according to an explicit logical scheme.
- Categories:
  - **National Bibliography:** Comprehensive legal deposit record of all books published within a nation (e.g., *Indian National Bibliography - INB*, 1957; *British National Bibliography - BNB*, 1950).
  - **Trade Bibliography:** Catalogs issued by book trade networks and publishing consortia (e.g., *Books in Print*, *Indian Books in Print*).
  - **Subject Bibliography:** Systematic lists of materials devoted to a specific discipline or topic.

#### 3. Reviews of Progress and State-of-the-Art Reports
- Critical syntheses and evaluative surveys of scientific literature published in a particular field over an annual or designated timeframe.
- *Examples:* *Annual Review of Information Science and Technology (ARIST)*, *Advances in Computers*.

#### 4. Textbooks, Treatises, and Monographs
- **Monograph:** A scholarly, exhaustive treatise on a single narrow specialized subject written by a specialist.
- **Treatise:** A formal, systematic exposition of the fundamental principles and established knowledge of an entire discipline.
- **Textbook:** A pedagogical tool presenting established, consensus-based principles of a subject tailored to an instructional curriculum.

## 5. Tertiary Sources of Information

> **Definition:** Tertiary sources are compiled from primary and secondary sources. They act as signposts, guiding researchers and information seekers to the primary and secondary literature where the required information resides.

### Detailed Breakdown of Tertiary Sources:

#### 1. Bibliography of Bibliographies
- As thousands of subject and national bibliographies proliferated worldwide, guidebooks listing and classifying bibliographies became essential.
- **Global Benchmark Work:**
  - Theodore Besterman: ***A World Bibliography of Bibliographies*** (First Edition published 1939–1940; 5 volumes).

#### 2. Guides to Reference Sources and Literature
- Comprehensive evaluative manuals detailing the best encyclopedias, dictionaries, bibliographies, directories, and online databases across diverse disciplines.
- **Premier Reference Guides:**
  - **Guide to Reference Books:** Originally conceived by Alice Bertha Kroeger (1902); subsequently edited by Constance M. Winchell, Eugene P. Sheehy, and Robert Balay; published by the American Library Association (ALA).
  - **Walford's Guide to Reference Material:** Compiled by Dr. Albert John Walford (A.J. Walford, 1959); published by the Library Association (UK).

#### 3. Directories of Directories
- Catalogs listing published trade, professional, institutional, and biographical directories.
- *Example:* *Directories in Print* (published by Gale Research).

#### 4. Yearbooks, Almanacs, and Institutional Directories
- Many library classificationists classify yearbooks (*Europa World Year Book*), almanacs (*Whitaker's Almanack*), and directories (*The World of Learning*) as tertiary sources due to their compiled nature and role as directories/indexes to institutions, organizations, and statistical bodies.

## 6. Non-Documentary Information Sources

Non-documentary sources represent information that is not formally recorded or published in physical or digital documents, but resides within human beings and institutional organizations:

```
                   Non-Documentary Sources
                         /        \
                        /          \
            Human Sources            Institutional Sources
      - Subject Experts              - Research & Development Laboratories
      - Professional Peers           - Universities and Academic Centers
      - Industrial Consultants       - Government Ministries & Statistical Bureaus
      - Invisible Colleges           - Learned Societies and Professional Bodies
```

### Invisible Colleges:
- Coined by **Derek J. de Solla Price** in his landmark book *"Little Science, Big Science"* (1963).
- Refers to an informal, elite communication network of leading scientists and researchers working at the cutting edge of a specialized discipline who communicate nascent findings directly through letters, drafts, emails, and closed seminars long before formal journal publication.
