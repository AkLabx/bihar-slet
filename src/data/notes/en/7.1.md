### Part 2: The Revolt of 1857 in Bihar

#### A. Commencement of the Uprising
1. **Rohini Village (Deoghar, then in Bihar):** On June 12, 1857, sepoys of the 32nd Native Infantry Regiment assassinated Major Norman Leslie, marking the beginning of the 1857 revolt in Bihar.
2. **Peer Ali's Uprising in Patna:** On July 3, 1857, **Peer Ali Khan**, a bookseller from Patna City, led a revolt against the British East India Company. British opium agent Dr. R. Lyell was killed. British Commissioner William Tayler suppressed the revolt and ordered the public execution of Peer Ali.
3. **Danapur Cantonment Revolt:** On July 25, 1857, native soldiers of the 7th, 8th, and 40th Regiments stationed at Danapur Cantonment mutinied and marched toward Jagdishpur (Arrah/Bhojpur) to unite with Babu Veer Kunwar Singh.

#### B. Babu Veer Kunwar Singh (1777–1858)
* **Role:** An 80-year-old chieftain and zamindar of Jagdishpur (Bhojpur/Arrah), who emerged as the legendary hero of the 1857 Indian rebellion in Bihar.
* **Guerrilla Warfare & Campaigns:** He liberated Arrah from British control and defeated British troops under Captain Dunbar. He then conducted extensive military campaigns across Rewa, Banda, Kanpur (supporting Nana Saheb), and Azamgarh, repeatedly outmaneuvering British forces.
* **Supreme Sacrifice:** On April 23, 1858, near Jagdishpur, Kunwar Singh routed the British army commanded by Captain Le Grand. While crossing the Ganga river under heavy British fire, a bullet shattered his left wrist; he severed his own injured hand and offered it to Mother Ganga to prevent infection. He succumbed to his battlefield injuries on April 26, 1858.
* **Vijayotsav (Victory Day):** April 23 is commemorated every year as **Vijay Diwas** (Victory Day) in honour of his historic triumph over the British.
* **Amar Singh:** Younger brother of Kunwar Singh who established a parallel underground administration in the Kaimur Hills and continued guerrilla resistance against the British after Kunwar Singh's demise.

### Part 3: Creation of Bihar as a Separate Province (1912)

* **Key Pioneers:** Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha and Mahesh Narayan. In 1894, they launched the newspaper **'Bihar Times'** to advocate the separation of Bihar from Bengal Presidency.
* **Delhi Durbar (December 12, 1911):** British Emperor King George V announced the annulment of the partition of Bengal and declared the creation of a separate province comprising Bihar and Orissa.
* **Official Notification:** The official notification carving out the new province of Bihar and Orissa was issued on **March 22, 1912**. Hence, **March 22** is celebrated annually as **'Bihar Diwas'**.
* **First Lieutenant Governor:** Sir Charles Stuart Bayley.
* **Patna High Court:** Established on **February 3, 1916** (First Chief Justice: Sir Edward Maynard des Champs Chamier; First Indian Chief Justice: Sir Syed Fazl Ali).
* **Patna University:** Established on **October 1, 1917** (First Vice-Chancellor: J.G. Jennings).
* **Separation of Orissa:** On April 1, 1936, Orissa was carved out as an independent province under the Government of India Act 1935.
* **Creation of Jharkhand:** On November 15, 2000, southern Bihar comprising 46% of its geographical area was carved out to create Jharkhand as India's 28th state.

### Part 4: Champaran Satyagraha (1917)

#### A. Background & The Tinkathia System
* **Tinkathia System:** European indigo planters forcibly mandated local ryots (farmers) to cultivate indigo on **3/20th of every bigha** (3 kattas out of 20 kattas) of their prime fertile land.
* **Economic Exploitation:** When chemical synthetic dyes were invented in Germany, demand for natural indigo plummeted. However, European planters imposed extortionate exactions, illegal cesses, and compensation taxes (*Tawan* and *Sharahbeshi*) on the impoverished peasants.

#### B. Arrival of Mahatma Gandhi & Historic Impact
1. **Invitation by Raj Kumar Shukla:** During the December 1916 Lucknow Session of the Indian National Congress, **Pandit Raj Kumar Shukla**, an indigo farmer from Murli Bharhawa village (Champaran), met Mahatma Gandhi and urged him to visit Champaran.
2. **Arrival:** Gandhi reached Patna on April 10, 1917, and proceeded to Motihari (Champaran) on April 15, 1917.
3. **Core Associates:** Dr. Rajendra Prasad, Anugrah Narayan Sinha, Acharya J.B. Kripalani, Brajkishore Prasad, Dharnidhar, Shambhu Sharan, and Mahadev Desai.
4. **Historical Significance:**
   * This was Mahatma Gandhi's **First Civil Disobedience / Satyagraha experiment in India**.
   * Lieutenant Governor Sir Edward Gait appointed the **Champaran Agrarian Enquiry Committee**, with Gandhi as an official member.
   * Based on the committee's recommendations, the Tinkathia system was formally abolished by law, and European planters were forced to refund **25% of the illegally extorted money** to the farmers.
   * Impressed by the moral victory of this movement, **Rabindranath Tagore** bestowed the title **'Mahatma'** upon Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi.

### Part 5: Khilafat, Non-Cooperation & Swaraj Movements (1919–1923)

#### A. Khilafat and Non-Cooperation Movement
* **Contribution of Maulana Mazharul Haque:**
  * Established **Sadaqat Ashram** on the banks of the Ganga in Patna (on land generously donated by Khairu Mian).
  * Founded and published the English daily newspaper **'The Motherland'** in September 1921 to propagate nationalistic ideas and the Non-Cooperation Movement.
* **Bihar Vidyapeeth:** Inaugurated by Mahatma Gandhi on February 6, 1921, to provide national education free from British government control. Maulana Mazharul Haque served as Chancellor and Dr. Brajkishore Prasad as Vice-Chancellor.
* **Boycott of Prince of Wales:** When the Prince of Wales visited Patna on December 22–23, 1921, the entire city observed an unprecedented total strike (hartal) displaying black flags.

#### B. Gaya Congress Session (1922)
* **President:** Deshbandhu **Chittaranjan Das (C.R. Das)**.
* **Formation of Swaraj Party:** Ideological divisions arose between "Pro-changers" (advocating council entry) and "No-changers". Following the session, C.R. Das and Motilal Nehru resigned from Congress positions to establish the Swaraj Party.
* **Swaraj Party in Bihar (February 1923):** In Bihar, the Swaraj Party branch was formed with **Narayan Prasad** as President and **Prof. Abdul Bari** as Secretary. **Dr. Shri Krishna Singh** led its legislative activities.

### Part 6: Simon Commission, Salt Satyagraha & Kisan Movement (1928–1939)

#### A. Boycott of the Simon Commission (1928)
* The all-British Simon Commission arrived in Patna on December 12, 1928. An all-party demonstration led by **Anugrah Narayan Sinha** greeted the commission with black flags and massive protests.

#### B. Salt Satyagraha & Civil Disobedience (1930)
* **Launch:** Commenced on April 15, 1930, in Champaran and Saran districts by manufacturing salt from saline soil.
* **Patna Salt Satyagraha:** On April 16, 1930, volunteers led by **Ambika Kant Sinha** violated the salt laws at 'Nakhas Pond' in Patna.
* **No-Chowkidari Tax Campaign:** Because Bihar is landlocked and lacked vast saline seashores, the primary and most powerful expression of the Civil Disobedience Movement in the province was the **refusal to pay Chowkidari Tax** (prominently sustained in Saran, Champaran, Munger, and Bhagalpur).
* **Naked Strike (Nangi Hartal):** Inmates of Chapra Jail refused to wear foreign-manufactured garments and protested naked until swadeshi khadi clothes were provided.

#### C. Bihar Provincial Kisan Sabha (BPKS)
* **Foundation:** Established in November 1929 at the historic Sonepur Cattle Fair.
* **Founding President:** **Swami Sahjanand Saraswati**; Secretary: Dr. Shri Krishna Singh.
* **Objective:** Abolition of the exploitative Zamindari system, occupancy rights for tenant farmers, and reduction of agrarian rents.
* **Bakasht Movement (1937–39):** Militant agrarian struggle led by **Karyanand Sharma** at Barahiya Tal (Munger) against the eviction of tenant farmers from Bakasht lands by oppressive landlords.
* **All India Kisan Sabha (AIKS):** Formed in April 1936 at Lucknow with Swami Sahjanand Saraswati as President and N.G. Ranga as General Secretary.

#### D. Bihar Socialist Party (1931/1934)
* **1931:** Foundation laid by Ganga Sharan Singh, Rambriksh Benipuri, and Ramanand Mishra.
* **Formal Establishment (May 1934):** Formally inaugurated at Anjuman Islamia Hall in Patna.
  * **President:** Acharya Narendra Deva.
  * **General Secretary:** **Jayaprakash Narayan (JP)**.

### Part 7: 1937 Provincial Elections & The First Congress Ministry

* **Elections under Government of India Act 1935:** Held in early 1937. The Congress won an absolute majority, capturing 98 out of 152 elected seats.
* **First Interim Premier:** On April 1, 1937, **Mohammad Yunus** (Independent Party) formed the first interim government and took oath as the first Premier of Bihar (heads of provincial governments were designated Premiers).
* **First Congress Ministry (July 20, 1937):**
  * **Premier:** **Dr. Shri Krishna Singh (Bihar Kesari)**.
  * **Deputy Premier cum Finance Minister:** **Dr. Anugrah Narayan Sinha (Bihar Vibhuti)**.
  * **Speaker of Legislative Assembly:** **Ram Dayalu Singh**; Deputy Speaker: Prof. Abdul Bari.
  * **President of Legislative Council:** Rajiv Ranjan Prasad.
* **Resignation (October 31, 1939):** The ministry resigned in protest against the unilateral decision of the British government to drag India into World War II without the consent of the Indian people.

### Part 8: Quit India Movement (1942) in Bihar

#### A. Patna Secretariat Firing (August 11, 1942)
* **The Incident:** Brave young students marched toward the Secretariat building in Patna to hoist the Indian National Tricolour atop the assembly building.
* **Firing Order:** Patna District Magistrate **W.G. Archer** ordered police to open fire on the unarmed students.
* **Martyrdom:** 7 young students were martyred on the spot, and 14 were critically wounded.
* **The Seven Martyrs:**
  1. Umakant Prasad Sinha (Class 9, Ram Mohan Roy Seminary)
  2. Ramanand Singh (Class 11, Ram Mohan Roy Seminary)
  3. Satish Prasad Jha (Class 11, Patna Collegiate School)
  4. Jagpati Kumar (2nd Year, B.N. College)
  5. Devipada Choudhury (Class 9, Miller High School)
  6. Rajendra Singh (Matriculation, Patna High School)
  7. Ramgovind Singh (Matriculation, Punpun High School)
* **Martyrs' Memorial (Shahid Smarak):** The foundation stone of the Martyrs' Memorial facing the Bihar Assembly was laid after independence by India's first President, Dr. Rajendra Prasad.

#### B. Jayaprakash Narayan & The 'Azad Dasta'
* **Escape from Hazaribagh Jail:** On the festival night of Diwali (November 9, 1942), **Jayaprakash Narayan**, along with Ramanand Mishra, Yogendra Shukla, Suraj Narayan Singh, and Gulab Chand Gupta, scaled the boundary walls of Hazaribagh Central Jail and escaped.
* **Formation of Azad Dasta:** Operating underground from the dense Rajvilas forests of the Nepal Terai, JP established the revolutionary guerrilla outfit **Azad Dasta**.
* **Military Training Director:** **Sardar Nityanand Singh** provided rigorous paramilitary and sabotage training to youth (disrupting railway tracks, cutting telegraph lines, burning government depots).
* **Radio Broadcasts:** Dr. Ram Manohar Lohia led the underground radio and propaganda division of Azad Dasta.

#### C. Parallel Governments (Prati Sarkar)
* **Sultanpur & Bhagalpur:** Underground resistance led by Parshuram Dal.
* **Siwan & Saran:** Parallel administration organized by Jaglal Chaudhary.
* **Dumraon (Buxar) & Munger:** British authority collapsed completely for weeks, replaced by local village councils and panchayat administration.
