## 1. International Standard Numbers

### (A) ISBN (International Standard Book Number)
- **International Standard:** Standardized under **ISO 2108**.
- **Historical Genesis:**
  - In **1966–1967**, British bookseller W. H. Smith, in collaboration with Prof. Gordon Foster of Trinity College Dublin, devised the 9-digit **Standard Book Numbering (SBN)** system.
  - The International Organization for Standardization (ISO) adapted SBN into the 10-digit **ISO 2108** standard in **1970**.
  - **Transition to 13 Digits:** On **January 1, 2007**, the 10-digit ISBN was globally expanded to a **13-digit ISBN** to align directly with the global EAN-13 (European/International Article Number) retail barcode standard.

#### The Five Structural Elements of a 13-Digit ISBN:
A 13-digit ISBN is divided into 5 distinct segments separated by four hyphens (e.g., `978-81-7000-123-4`):
1. **GS1 Prefix Element:** 3 digits — Currently restricted to **`978`** or **`979`** (indicating Bookland in GS1 international barcoding).
2. **Registration Group / Country Element:** 1 to 5 digits — Identifies the country, language area, or geographical region.
   - *Designated Country Identifiers for India:* **`81`** or **`93`**. (e.g., English-speaking regions: 0 or 1; France: 2; Germany: 3).
3. **Registrant / Publisher Element:** Identifies the specific publishing house or imprint.
4. **Publication / Title Element:** Identifies the specific title, volume, or binding edition issued by that publisher.
5. **Check Digit:** The final single digit calculated using **Modulus 10** with alternating weights of **1 and 3**, mathematically verifying data entry integrity.
   - *(Historical Note: The legacy 10-digit ISBN utilized a Modulus 11 check digit calculation, where the Roman numeral **'X'** represented the numerical value of 10).*

#### National ISBN Agency for India:
- **Raja Rammohun Roy National Agency for ISBN (RRRNA):**
  - Operating under the Department of Higher Education, Ministry of Education, Government of India (New Delhi).
  - The sole statutory national agency authorized to allocate ISBN blocks to authors, institutions, and commercial publishers across India free of charge through its online portal.

---

### (B) ISSN (International Standard Serial Number)
- **International Standard:** Governed by **ISO 3297** (first approved in 1975).
- **Scope:** An unambiguous, 8-digit international identifier for serial publications, journals, periodicals, newspapers, monographic series, and annuals across print and digital media.
- **Structural Layout:** Composed of exactly **8 digits** divided into two equal 4-digit groups separated by a hyphen (e.g., `ISSN 1234-5679` or `ISSN 0970-1052`):
  - The first 7 digits constitute the sequential, unique title identification code.
  - The 8th digit is a **Check Digit** calculated on a **Modulus 11** algorithm (if the remainder is 10, the check digit is officially assigned as the uppercase Roman numeral **'X'**).
- **Media Variations:**
  - **P-ISSN:** Assigned to physical print editions.
  - **E-ISSN:** Assigned to electronic and online digital editions.
- **National ISSN Centre for India:**
  - **CSIR-NIScPR (National Institute of Science Communication and Policy Research, formerly NISCAIR / INSDOC), New Delhi:**
  - Headquartered at the National Science Library (NSL) in New Delhi; acts as the exclusive national nodal agency responsible for assigning ISSNs to serials published in India.

## 2. Copyright Legislation

> **Definition:** Copyright is an exclusive, statutory legal right granted by law to the creator of an original intellectual, literary, dramatic, musical, or artistic work, protecting the expression of their ideas against unauthorized reproduction, publishing, distribution, translation, adaptation, or commercial exploitation.

### International Copyright Treaties:
1. **Berne Convention (1886):**
   - The foundational international convention for the protection of literary and artistic works, signed in Berne, Switzerland.
   - *Key Legal Principle:* **Automatic Protection** — copyright protection is secured instantly upon creation of the work without requiring mandatory formal registration or deposit.
2. **Universal Copyright Convention (UCC):**
   - Adopted at Geneva in **1952** under the auspices of UNESCO (introducing the recognized copyright symbol: ©).
3. **World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO):**
   - Established on **July 14, 1967** pursuant to the Stockholm Convention; became a specialized agency of the United Nations (UN) in **1974**.
   - Headquarters: **Geneva, Switzerland**.
4. **TRIPS Agreement (1995):**
   - The Agreement on Trade-Related Aspects of Intellectual Property Rights, administered by the World Trade Organization (WTO).

### The Indian Copyright Act, 1957:
- **Enactment:** Passed by the Indian Parliament in 1957; came into statutory effect nationwide on **January 21, 1958**.
- **Major Legislative Amendments:** Amended in 1983, 1984, 1992, 1994, 1999, and comprehensively in **2012** (introducing protections for digital rights management, technological protection measures, statutory licensing, and lifetime royalties for music composers and lyricists).

#### Statutory Term of Copyright Protection in India:
- **Literary, Dramatic, Musical, and Artistic Works:**
  - **Lifetime of the author + 60 Years** (calculated from the beginning of the calendar year following the author's death).
- **Cinematograph Films, Sound Recordings, Photographs, Posthumous Publications, Anonymous / Pseudonymous Works, and Works of Government / International Organizations:**
  - Protected for **60 Years** from the beginning of the calendar year following the date of first publication.

---

### Fair Dealing Exemptions (Section 52)
From the standpoint of library operations, academic research, and public education, the most vital statutory limitation to copyright exclusivity is the doctrine of **Fair Dealing**:
- Under **Section 52** of the Indian Copyright Act, 1957, specified acts do not constitute copyright infringement:
  1. **Private Personal Study and Research:** Fair copying of limited extracts by students, scholars, and researchers for non-commercial academic research.
  2. **Criticism or Review:** Quoting passages from a work for analytical review or literary critique.
  3. **Current Affairs Reporting:** Excerpts used in print journalism, broadcasting, or news reporting.
  4. **Judicial and Educational Use:** Reproduction for legislative or judicial proceedings, and reproduction of questions in educational examination papers.
  5. **Library Preservation:** The making of up to **three copies** of an out-of-print, damaged, or unprocurable book by the librarian of a non-profit public library for long-term preservation.

---

### Creative Commons (CC Licenses)
A flexible legal licensing framework designed to facilitate open access and legal sharing of creative and scholarly works across the digital commons.
- **Founder:** Established in **2001** by American legal scholar **Lawrence Lessig**.
- **Four Core License Conditions:**
  1. **BY (Attribution):** Licensees must give appropriate credit to the original creator.
  2. **SA (ShareAlike):** Derived works must be distributed under the exact same or compatible license.
  3. **NC (Non-Commercial):** The work may not be used for commercial advantage or monetary compensation.
  4. **ND (NoDerivatives):** The original work may not be modified, altered, adapted, or transformed.
- **CC0 (Public Domain Dedication):** The creator completely surrenders all copyright interests, releasing the work unconditionally into the worldwide public domain.

## 3. Types of Intellectual Property Rights (IPR)

Intellectual Property Rights (IPR) are legal protections granted over creations of the human intellect:

| Category of IPR | Governing Indian Legislation | Statutory Term of Protection | Protected Subject Matter & Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Patents** | The Patents Act, 1970 (Amended 2005) | **20 Years** (from date of filing) | New technological inventions, industrial processes, and products |
| **Copyright** | The Copyright Act, 1957 | **Life of author + 60 Years** | Books, articles, drama, music, fine art, computer software |
| **Trademarks** | The Trade Marks Act, 1999 | **10 Years** (indefinitely renewable) | Distinctive brand names, commercial logos, slogans, and trade dress |
| **Geographical Indications (GI)** | GI of Goods Act, 1999 | **10 Years** (renewable) | Products possessing unique regional qualities; India's 1st GI: **Darjeeling Tea (2004)** |
| **Industrial Designs** | The Designs Act, 2000 | **10 Years** (+5 years extension = 15 years) | Aesthetic exterior shape, pattern, ornamentation, or surface design of objects |

## 4. Professional Code of Ethics for Librarians

A professional Code of Ethics defines the core normative values, professional commitments, and moral principles that guide information professionals in serving users, institutions, and democratic society.

### (A) Dr. S. R. Ranganathan's Ethical Precepts:
Dr. S. R. Ranganathan formulated foundational ethical imperatives for library professionals:
1. **Universal Service Orientation:** Providing unbiased, equal service to every reader regardless of social class, caste, religion, or gender.
2. **Custodial Integrity:** Safeguarding rare manuscripts and documentary heritage as sacred cultural trusts.
3. **Continuous Professional Competence:** Maintaining relentless dedication to acquiring mastery over modern library theories, indexing standards, and emerging technologies.

### (B) ALA Code of Ethics (1939):
The American Library Association (ALA) formally adopted its first official Code of Ethics in **1939** (subsequently revised in 1981, 1995, 2008, and 2021). Its core pillars include:
- **Intellectual Freedom:** Defending the fundamental right of every individual to read and access diverse viewpoints while actively opposing intellectual censorship.
- **Patron Privacy and Confidentiality:** Strictly protecting the confidentiality of library patron circulation records, reading inquiries, and personal search histories.
- **Equity of Access:** Ensuring equitable, unimpeded access to information resources for all community members without discrimination.

### (C) IFLA Code of Ethics for Librarians (2012):
Approved in **2012** by the International Federation of Library Associations and Institutions (IFLA) in The Hague, this global code outlines six core professional pillars:
1. **Access to Information:** Ensuring transparency, authenticity, and unrestricted access to knowledge.
2. **Responsibilities Towards Individuals and Society:** Promoting social inclusion, digital literacy, and accessibility for marginalized groups.
3. **Privacy, Secrecy, and Transparency:** Upholding user confidentiality while promoting transparent institutional operations.
4. **Open Access and Intellectual Property:** Advocating open access while scrupulously observing intellectual property and fair dealing.
5. **Neutrality, Personal Integrity, and Professional Skills:** Refusing to let personal biases compromise the neutrality of collection development and user services.
6. **Colleague and Professional Relationships:** Fostering mutual respect, workplace collegiality, and active participation in professional library associations.
